01 — Overview

The house rules.

We make films that feel lived-in. Our brand should feel the same way — confident, cinematic, a little warm around the edges, and always in service of the story.

Cinematic, not decorative

Every design choice earns its place through clarity, contrast, and intention.

Human scale

Big type, strong characters, warm neutrals. Designed to be felt, not just read.

Editorially confident

We mix a geometric wordmark with a literary italic — structure meets story.

One system, many rooms

Social, web, print, and on-set all speak the same language.

How to use this hub Treat each section as a contract. Designers and collaborators should be able to produce an on-brand asset using only the tokens, rules, and samples in this document. If a need isn't covered here, extend the system — don't bypass it. This document is the source of truth.
02 — Logo System

The mark.

A geometric "HP" monogram fused with a cinematic play-triangle. One gesture, two meanings: initials and ignition.

Primary · on paper
Reversed · on stage black
On spotlight orange
On curtain plum
CLEARSPACE = 1× MONOGRAM HEIGHT
Do

Clearspace

Always maintain clearspace equal to the height of the monogram triangle on all sides. No text, border, or image may intrude.

MIN DIGITAL 64PX · PRINT 18MM
Do

Minimum sizes

Full lockup: 64px / 18mm wide. Monogram-only: 32px / 10mm. Below this, legibility of the "PRODUCTIONS" line drops.

USE ONLY APPROVED TOKENS
Do

Color behavior

Render the logo in Stage Black, Paper, Spotlight Orange, Curtain Plum, or pure white only. Never tint, gradient, or photo-fill the mark.

NEVER
Don't

Never distort

Do not stretch, skew, rotate beyond horizontal, outline, drop-shadow, or separate the monogram from the wordmark in secondary use.

NEVER
Don't

Never swap typography

The wordmark is typeset and locked. Never re-set "PALMER HOUSE PRODUCTIONS" in a different font or weight as a substitute logo.

NEVER
Don't

Never place on chaos

Do not place the mark on busy photography without an overlay or a clean plate. Always meet AA contrast against the background.

06 — Voice & Tone

How we sound.

Confident but never cold. Editorial but never precious. Cinematic but never self-serious.

Voice attributes

Cinematic
Write like the camera is rolling. Favor present tense, sensory detail, and the specific over the generic.
Warm
Contractions are welcome. First person plural ("we") for the studio, second person ("you") for the reader.
Confident
State the work plainly. No hedging, no "we hope." Replace "innovative solutions" with what we actually did.
Editorial
Use a pull quote or an em-dash aside where a lesser writer would use a bullet list.

Tone by context

Homepage / hero
Short, cinematic, a single clear claim. One sentence and a verb.
Case studies
Narrative first person. Set the scene, state the problem, show the frame, credit the crew.
Social captions
One line of mood + one line of credit. No emojis unless the post is playful and intentional.
Email / client
Direct, warm, signed. Greet by first name. Never open with "I hope this finds you well."
SAY — ✓

"We shoot on location in Brooklyn. We cut in-house. We ship on time."

"A three-part documentary about the quiet economies of Crown Heights."

"Reel Pal handles the content so the founder can handle the company."

DON'T SAY — ×

"We leverage best-in-class cinematic solutions to amplify your brand narrative."

"Innovative storytelling for today's digital-first world."

"World-class premium content that drives results."

07 — Imagery & Motion

The look.

Photography, illustration, and motion all answer to the same director: shape, contrast, and the quiet moment before the action.

Photography direction

01

Anamorphic eye

Favor 2.39:1 or 16:9 crops, shallow depth of field, warm highlights, and a subject that breathes with the frame.

02

Lived-in light

Golden hour, practicals, window light. Avoid flat studio lighting unless the subject demands clinical clarity.

03

Crew and craft

Behind-the-scenes gets equal billing. Show hands on gear, gaffer tape, a crew laughing between takes.

04

Honest faces

Real people, real eye contact. No stock smiles. Every portrait should look like a still from a scene.

Motion principles

01

Cut on emotion

Edits land on a breath, not a beat. Never cut to fill time — let a frame hold.

02

Typography moves

Display type animates with easing (cubic-bezier .2, .8, .2, 1), 400–600ms. Never pops or bounces.

03

Score, don't soundtrack

Music supports story, never carries it. If the film works on mute, the music is doing its job.

04

Endframe always

Every piece of motion closes on the logo lockup, Stage Black, with a 1.5s hold. No exceptions.
